[AI Synthesis]: A psychological game is an ongoing series of complementary ulterior transactions progressing to a well-defined, dramatic payoff. People play for strokes, structure, and script confirmation; intimacy is rare and risky, so games fill most serious social life. The practical skill is knowing when to stop.

### Definition
* A game is a recurring set of transactions, often repetitious, superficially plausible, with a concealed motivation—a series of moves with a snare or gimmick.
* Distinguished from procedures, rituals, and pastimes by (1) ulterior quality and (2) the payoff.
* Procedures, rituals, and pastimes are candid; every game is basically dishonest, and the ending is dramatic rather than merely exciting.
* "Game" and "play" do not imply fun; they can be grim or fatal (Alcoholic, War).

### Why people play
* There is little opportunity for intimacy; intense intimacy is psychologically impossible for most people—so serious social life runs on games.
* Advantages: biological stroking, confirmation of existential position, internal and external psychological economy, structuring time with intimates and outsiders.
* Games are dynamic parts of an unconscious life-plan (script), filling time while advancing toward a constructive miracle or a destructive catastrophe.
* Some people's psychic stability depends on their games; stripping them away can plunge them into despair or psychosis—analyze with prudence.

### Analysis grid
* Formal description uses: Thesis, Antithesis, Aim, Roles, Dynamics, childhood prototype, Transactional Paradigm (social vs psychological level), Moves, Advantages.
* Antithesis = refuse to play or undercut the payoff; "it" intensifies, then may fall into despair (Adult bewilderment—distinct from Child-led depression). Laughter is the opposite of despair.
* Degrees: first-degree (socially acceptable), second-degree (no permanent damage, but players prefer to hide it), third-degree (played for keeps—surgery, courtroom, or morgue).

### Significance
* Historical: games pass generation to generation.
* Cultural: raising children is largely teaching them which games to play.
* Social: sandwiched between pastimes and intimacy—compromise when intimacy feels dangerous.
* Personal: people choose friends who play the same games; changing games risks extrusion from one circle and welcome in another.

### Player types
* In any game, professionals know when to quit; amateurs do not.
* The sulker is someone angry at mother—Parent/Child material kept in play rather than resolved.
* The "weirdo" is overly sensitive to Parent ego-state influence—social oddness as a defense against scripted norms.

### Thesaurus (sketch)
* Life: Alcoholic, Debtor, Kick Me, NIGYSOB, See What You Made Me Do.
* Marital: Corner, Courtroom, If It Weren't for You, Look How Hard I've Tried, and others.
* Party: Ain't It Awful, Blemish, Schlemiel, Why Don't You – Yes But.
* Also sexual, underworld, consulting-room, and a few "good" games.

### Actions
* When a recurring argument or mood loop feels familiar, ask what game is running and what payoff each side is collecting.
* Practice stopping earlier than the script demands—treat quitting as skill, not defeat.
* Test an antithesis once: refuse the usual move or undercut the payoff; notice intensification, then despair or laughter.
* Do not strip someone's stabilizing game without a safer intimacy or Adult substitute available.
