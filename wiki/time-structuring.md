[AI Synthesis]: People structure waking time to meet three hungers—stimulation, recognition (strokes), and structure—moving from activities and rituals through pastimes and games; only intimacy fully satisfies all three.

### Strokes and social recognition
* The form of stimulation people most prefer is physical intimacy.
* A stroke is recognition of another's presence—the fundamental unit of social action.
* Colloquial rule: if you are not stroked, your spinal cord will shrivel up.
* After infancy, physical intimacy becomes scarce; stimulus-hunger partly transforms into recognition-hunger (even a nod can count).

### Three hungers
* After hunger for stimulation and recognition comes hunger for structure—how to fill waking hours.
* Stimulation and recognition ward off sensory and emotional deprivation.
* Structure expresses the need to avoid boredom; prolonged boredom equals emotional starvation.
* Intimacy is the only answer that can fully satisfy all three; its prototype is loving impregnation.
* Pastimes and games are substitutes for the real living of real intimacy.

### Hierarchy of social time
* In order of complexity among people: (1) Rituals (2) Pastimes (3) Games (4) Intimacy (5) Activity as a matrix for any of the others.
* Social contact advantages: relief of tension, avoidance of noxious situations, procurement of stroking, maintenance of equilibrium.

### Procedures and rituals
* A procedure is a series of simple complementary Adult transactions aimed at manipulating reality (data processing and probability); Adult programs it.
* A ritual is a stereotyped series of simple complementary transactions programmed by external social forces—Parentally patterned tradition and manners.
* Both are stereotyped: once started, the series is predictable to a foreordained end.
* Greeting rituals are stroke accounting (e.g. Hi–Hi as a two-stroke ritual; long absence requires catching up the "debt").

### Pastimes
* A pastime is a semi-ritualistic series of complementary transactions around one field of material, whose primary object is to structure an interval of time.
* Typical at parties and waiting periods before meetings.
* Pastimes serve as social selection: the Child assesses who is eligible for more complex relationships—games.
* They also confirm role and stabilize existential position (often fixed between ages 1–7).
* Pastimes grow boring with repetition; intimacy feels dangerous—so most people compromise on games.

### Actions
* Notice which hunger a conversation or habit is really feeding—stimulation, stroke, or structure—and whether a ritual has replaced genuine contact.
* Prefer direct Adult exchanges (procedures) when the goal is information; recognize when Parent-scripted rituals are carrying the social load.
* At gatherings, watch how pastimes sort companions into future game partners.
