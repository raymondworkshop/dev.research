[AI Synthesis]: Listening transforms the talk: they listen better when you listen, and you influence more when you understand what drives them. Curiosity must be authentic; manage your internal voice; inquire to learn; paraphrase; acknowledge feelings before problem-solving.

### Listening transforms
* Learn about the other person; listening to them helps them listen to you.
* Listen for feelings underneath their views — frustration, pride, fear — and acknowledge those feelings.
* Listening well enhances your ability to influence: you understand hesitations, hopes, interests, and concerns so you can address what matters most to them.

### Stance of curiosity
* Forget the words; focus on authenticity — listen because you are curious and care, not because you are supposed to.
* What communicates most powerfully is whether you are genuinely curious and care about what they think and feel. False intentions are not fixed by careful wording.
* Manage your internal voice: find and listen to it first; only when aware of your own thoughts and feelings can you focus on them.
* Stay on the purpose of understanding why they see it differently; if that fails, express your internal voice before trying to listen — “I’m surprised… I think I disagree, but first, say more about how you see it.”

### Inquiry, paraphrase, acknowledge
* Ask questions only to learn more; don’t make statements disguised as questions.
* Share your view as an observation, then ask for their reaction; prefer open-ended questions; give them the choice whether to answer.
* Paraphrase to check understanding and show you’ve heard.
* Feelings crave acknowledgment — acknowledge before problem-solving; acknowledging is not agreeing. You can honor the power of the feelings while disagreeing with the substance.
* Whether to listen is a strategic choice: often the issue is managing competing rights, views, or needs that are all more or less legitimate.

### Actions
* Listen from curiosity and care, not as a tactic.
* If your internal voice is loud, name it briefly, then return to their story.
* Ask to learn; paraphrase; acknowledge feelings before solving.
