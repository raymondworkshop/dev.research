[AI Synthesis]: Every hard talk is three conversations at once — What Happened, Feelings, and Identity. Move from arguing who’s right toward a learning conversation: understand their view, explain yours, share feelings, and manage the problem together.

### What Happened
* Structure around truth, intentions, and blame — often “I am right, and you are wrong.”
* These talks are not about what is true; they are about what is important.
* Understanding perceptions, interpretations, and values of both sides moves you from delivering messages toward asking questions and offering your views as perceptions — not as “the truth.”
* The Intention Invention: we assume intentions from other people’s behavior.
* The Blame Frame: focusing on a joint contribution system lets you learn the inputs to the problem and work on correcting them.

### Different stories
* People almost never change without first feeling understood.
* We each see the world differently: we notice different things, have different interpretations, and our conclusions reflect self-interest.
* Your task is understanding them better — making sense of the world as they see it. Understanding does not require agreeing or giving up your own story.

### Feelings and Identity
* The Feelings Conversation and the Identity Conversation run alongside What Happened.
* The Identity Conversation is about what you are saying to yourself about you. Anytime a conversation feels difficult, it is in part because it is about You — something beyond the apparent substance is at stake.

### Toward a learning conversation
* Understand what has happened from the other person’s point of view, explain yours, share and understand feelings, and work together to manage the problem going forward.
* That makes it more likely they will be open to being persuaded, and that you will learn something that changes how you understand the problem.

### Actions
* Before you argue facts, notice which of the three you are in — What Happened, Feelings, or Identity.
* Ask questions; explore how each person is making sense of the world.
* Offer your views as perceptions, interpretations, and values — not as the truth.
* Aim to understand their story without needing to agree with it.
