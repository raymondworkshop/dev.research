[AI Synthesis]: You are entitled to speak — your views and feelings are as legitimate as theirs. Say what is at the heart of the matter directly; tell your story with clarity, not as Absolute Truth; help them understand you.

### Finding your voice
* Recognizing your entitlement is a first step when you feel frightened or powerless.
* My views and feelings are as legitimate, valuable, and important as yours — no more, but no less. We all deserve respect and dignity.
* Silences had not protected you.
* Beware self-sabotage: trapped between “I should stand up” and a hidden feeling that you don’t deserve to be heard — whose past voice says you’re not entitled? When do you assume your thoughts matter less?
* Most people prefer someone who will speak their mind; relationships often grow when each person feels safe to be themselves and seen for who they are.
* Sometimes we don’t express ourselves because we assume it’s not our place — work to make the space safe and inviting for everyone to share.

### Speak the heart of the matter
* Start with what matters most: “Have I said what is at the heart of the matter for me? Have I shared what is at stake?”
* Say what you mean; don’t make them guess — figure out what you really think and feel, then say it directly.
* Use the “me-me and”: each of your various perceptions, feelings, and assumptions is important; don’t simplify your story into one flat claim.

### Tell your story with clarity
* Don’t present conclusions as the Truth — “My view is that…”; explain why you think something is inappropriate; be clear about the nature of what you’re asserting.
* Share where conclusions come from: specific information, past experiences that shape how you’re thinking.
* Don’t exaggerate with “always” and “never”; leave room for them to change.
* They’ll need your help understanding you: different people take information differently; ask them to paraphrase; ask how they see it differently and why.
* The secret of powerful expression: you are the ultimate authority on you. Speak fully the range of your experience and you will be clear; speak for yourself and you speak with power.

### Actions
* Ask: have I said what is at the heart for me? If not, find the courage to try.
* Prefer “My view is that…” plus the information and experience behind it.
* Ask how they see it differently — and why — after you’ve spoken.
