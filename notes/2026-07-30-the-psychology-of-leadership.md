## The Psychologyof Leadership  
* That time, **I played to the absolute best of my abilities**    

*  A leader's job is to **inspire others**    
    - **You put on your oxygen mask before helping others**  
        - **Understand yourself** so you can understand others.
    It's about **empathy**, **leading by example**, and **personal resilience**.  
    - **focusing on mastery** over ego, **the importance of relationships and community**, the meaning of sport as a **meritocracy**, and engagement and flow  


### P1 - measurable goals  

* They're **working hard** and **persisting through difficulties** 
because of **their internal desire to control their lives**, **learn about the world**,
and **accomplish somethign that endures**    

* The leader's job is to **set goals for their organization and individuals**  
    - **measurable goals aer essential in business and life**    
    - goals enhance communication, collaboration, and performance evaluations. they motivate and align organizations   
    - the goal will serve to organize and measure the best of our energies and skills  

* improve your mental game  
    - **systematize warning signals**, encourage preventative behaviors, and **exit based on stop-loss rules**  
    - I **stess about the outcomes**, and I **also focus on what my team can control: our process**  

    - **differentiate between luck and skill**. make sure you **examine your decision-making process**    

### P2 **Happiness leads to success**  
*  Companies should **max profits for shareholders** as long as they also casre for employees, clients, and the environment  

* Happiness comes from positive emotions + Engagement/flow,  relationships, meaning, and accomplishments  
    - maintain positive relationships with people around them  
    - engage in tasks that are challenging yet match their capabilities  
    - find meaning and accomplishment in their work  

### P3  **Set Goals that max engagement**    
* **Ask yourself, what activities yield the greatest return on your time** ?   
    -  by estimating how much it contributes to his goal of making money for hsi clients  
    - **focusing on high return on time spent** activities are more likely to **align with your long-term goals and values**    


* **Applying ROTS as a leader**     
    - focus on high ROTS meeting  

    - find flow in all aspects of your life  

* Your goals should be **clear, and the rewards or feedback should be well-defined**     
    - The business world, is full of randomness, which means non-linear goals  
    - **SMART goals should be specific, measurable, achievalbe, realistic, and time-based**. Each of these elements max flow  

### P4 **Prioritize Relationships**    
* whether people emphasized **positive working relationships**    
    - collaboration and mutual respect  
    - elite athletes value two things in a coach: 
    **feeling that they care about you as a person**, not just an athlete,
    and **applicable feedback** that leads to improvement  

* with clear rules of the game and genuine respect among team members  
    -  Explain that you're about to **debate ideas, nothing personal**    
    -  before you disagree, take a second to **point out sth you agree on**.  
    "**We all agree that** ..., **but where I see things differently is** ..." 

* foster a culture where people **ask for feedback**    
    - effective collaboration is about **being able to disagree with mutual respect**    

* **Culture drives everything**    
    - Culture is **the shared set of norms, beliefs, and expected behaviors** in a group of people.  
    - **safety, belonging, trust, and shared vulnerability** are essential building blocks of this type of **family culture**.  
    - **culture is more about what you do** than what you say  

* be mindful of **role clarity**  
    - **who does what**? when teams aren't clear on who owns what, conflicts arise.
        + rethinking some people's job definition or reporting lines,
        as well as hiring and firing decisions  

    - remind people that they have incentives to work together  

    - **deliver the best the firm can offer** this client  
        + **what do the client need**?  based on this, we may recommend ... 

* Communication is essential, but **culture is more about what you do than what you say**    

* your most important life goal should be to **nurture positive relationships**  
    - ask about the culture and listen carefully to how people respond on collaborative company culture  
        + ask for examples of corporate decisions that illustrate a positive culture  

    - If I had to run a company on three measures, those measures would be **customer satisfaction, employee satisfaction, and cash flow**    

### P5 pursue goals that have meaning  
*   **emphasize your why**    
    - **ask the big questions**. You might realize your work has more purpose than you thought   
    
* **how you choose to define your work**    
    - their leaders **set big goals**. They **create a sense of direction**.
    They focus on a worthy mission  

### P6 define **accomplishment as a function of mastery goals**    
* Your psyche **combines a drive towards mastery** and **a desire to win**  
    - differentiate between winning at all costs and a broader notion of accomplishment  

* define **accomplishment as a function of mastery goals**    
    - **Mastery is all about process**.  It means **making how you do things better**   
        -  Play to improve, to learn, to solve problems, and to be in flow.
    when they win, it's great. But when they lose - **as long as they played well** - it's almost as great  
    
    - the mastery-focused player **enjoys the game a lot more**. They're better equipped to cope with mistakes.    
        -  their goal to improve their swing and derives personal satisfaction from it.
        this player plays for the sake of playing.  
        - the other player wants to impress others with a low handicap. 
        She plays for the sake of winning.  


### P7 how they approach their work that makes them role models  
* Your effectiveness as a leader depends in part on your ability to understand people's **motivations and mindsets**    
    - **ask them what they like most about their jobs**    

* **how they approach their work**?  
    - who's for the accomplishment orientation?  
    who's not focused on external rewards like fame and money?  
    who keeps their ego in check and does good work every day?  

    - Intrinsic motivation and big goals are great,
    but only if they don't come at the expense of **your mental and physical health**,
    your sense of balance, **strong family bonds**, and **positive and productive working relationships**    

* expand your definition of compensation  
    - competitive levels and differentiate based on performance  
    - additional factors such as autonomy, power, prestige, the social aspects of work, or altruism  

### P8 - Build a bigger rocket - unleash the power of social comparison  

* **Social comparison** is a powerful motivator for individuals and organizations  
    - It is in their power to **not want what they donot have**, and **to cheerfully put to good use what they have**  
        - they rarely spend enough time reminding everyong of what makes the team great,
        why the organization is lucky to benefit from competitive advantages, or why their culture is special. Even in tough times, or especially in tough times, **gratitude lifts morale**.  

*  **Internal competition should be secondary to collaboration** - or even a tool for it  
    - Make internal competition friendly and enjoyable  
        - Internal competition **must not come at the expense of  trust, mutual respect,
        and a shared understanding of the team's mission**  
    - **Rally your team** against a worthy competitor 
        - Most people **get more motivation through competition** than positive encouragement  


### P9 - Upgrade Your Operating System - identify and manage your core beliefs  
* know yourself better  
    - understand your roots and define your belief systems  

* Core beliefs  
    - when you feel deeply about something  

* Go ahead and **explore your childhood and traumatic or memorable life events** if needed. **Managing and upgrading your core beliefs will help you avoid frequent errors in judgment**, better allocate your time and energy, and ultimately, become a better leader

* **find a mentor you can talk to about your challenges**

    - **ask them to provide a clinical, unbiased assessment of your challenges**    

* **Stay close to revenues**

* **Build your human capital**

    - you progress towards creating, innovating, leading, selling, or risk-taking  

*  adjust your perspective
    - your expetation impact your happiness.
    manage them carefully  

*  **emphasize communication**    
    - in corporate life, **collaboration and teamwork** are essential.

## Unleashing the Power of Personality Psychology  
* Most of my job as a leader has been focused on relationships:
    - inspiring and helping people **find their motivation**, 
    **consoling** those who feel down about their work,
    and the hardest part of my job, **resolving conflicts** 

* **Empathy** is the key  
    - you must **listen and understand the perspective of others**,
    especially if it's different from yours.
    **Understand why people think what they think and do what they do**.
    - **All behavior is purposeful**. If someone's behavior doesn't make sense to you,
    that's often because you have not taken the time to **explore the underlying reasons for it**  
     

* Big Five traits  
    - extraversion  
    - agreeableness  
    - neuroticism   
    - openness  
    - conscientiousness  

### P14  Read the Room - call on the introverts  
* stop the extroverts and **call on introverts**  
    - apologize for interrupting, summarize the person's point, and pivot  
    - "So, Susanne, excellent points. **Apologies for interrupting**,
    **but we only have ten minutes left** in this meeting. **What you're saying is** ...;
    John, **what do you think**?"  

* listening is a superpower  
    - listen, then **summarize well and beiefly**  

### P15 Agreeableness is a desirable personality trait, being disagreeable sometimes  
* balance agreeableness with the courage to make unpopular yet necessary decisions  

* Agreeableness is **the willingness to go along and get along** - not having to have it your way    
    - if you're too agreeable, some people will take advantage of you.
    There are advantages to **a bit of disagreeableness**.  
    
* be disagreeable about 10% of the time.
    - **learn to pick your battles**  
    - you're pushing people to grow, especially if you're **giving them feedback in a respectful way** that 
    lets them know they're valeud
